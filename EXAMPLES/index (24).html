The Whole Community Solution Framework

Nine pillars. One system. Every community need addressed simultaneously.

Most programs solve for one problem. A clinic solves for health. A workforce program solves for employment. A housing project solves for shelter. The WCS Framework is different — it solves for all nine dimensions of community wellbeing at once, through a structured network deployed by a single trusted relationship: the Community Health Worker.

The Ecosystem

Five entities. One integrated system. Built to serve the community — and sustain itself.

Each entity has a distinct legal structure, a distinct role, and distinct revenue streams. They are designed to operate as one — and to protect each principal individually. Together they make possible what none could accomplish alone.

The physical container that makes the whole system possible.

What ASG activates

The community operating system that makes the campus more than housing.

The clinical engine — primary care, HEDIS gap closure, care plans, Medicaid billing, and the NP license that makes CHW services reimbursable. The clinic is where healthcare meets the community.

What the MSO manages

WCS Health Management LLC — the for-profit management services organization co-owned by the founder and Anthony — handles the non-clinical administrative infrastructure that supports the clinic and the broader ecosystem.

What the IP LLC protects

The founder's personal IP LLC owns the WCS Framework and licenses it to ASG CDC. Every campus, every new market, every organization that adopts the framework generates licensing income that belongs to the founder.

The Hidden ROI — Value-Added Benefits Activation

MCOs already paid for these benefits.
Almost nobody is using them.

Every Medicaid managed care organization prices value-added benefits into the per-member-per-month rate they receive from the state. They budget for them. They negotiate contracts around them. And in most markets, the vast majority of those benefits sit unclaimed — because no one ever told the member they existed. The CHW changes that entirely.

The reality most MCOs live with

Most Medicaid members use their insurance card twice a year. Once for a sick visit. Once for a prescription.

That is the entire relationship most members have with their health plan. The gym membership goes unused. The OTC allowance expires. The transportation benefit sits unclaimed. The prenatal support program nobody enrolled in. The tutoring benefit no parent ever knew about. The meal delivery service. The Costco membership. The doula services. The home safety equipment for seniors.

These benefits were priced into the PMPM. The MCO already paid for them. They generate no outcome. They improve no quality score. They reduce no utilization. They simply expire — unused, every single year, for every member who never knew they existed.

What the CHW does differently

The CHW studies the plan. Knows every benefit. Activates every one for every member — personally, in their home language, in their community.

This is not outreach. It is not a flyer. It is not a robocall. The CHW sits with the member, opens the plan documentation, and walks through every benefit the member is entitled to — one by one, until every benefit that applies is activated. Then the CHW follows up to make sure it was actually used.

For the MCO, this is not a new cost. The CHW is converting sunk cost into measurable return. Every benefit activated is a dollar that was already spent — now producing an outcome instead of expiring unused.

What members discover they already had — and never used
Gym memberships
Free or subsidized gym and fitness access. Reduces chronic disease risk, improves mental health, lowers long-term utilization. Already in the plan. Almost never activated.
OTC allowances
Monthly over-the-counter benefit cards — pain relievers, vitamins, first aid, personal care, sometimes groceries. Hundreds of dollars per year per member sitting in the plan, unclaimed.
Prenatal support
Prenatal vitamins, doula services, baby supplies, maternal health coaching, breastfeeding support. Among the most valuable benefits in any Medicaid plan — and among the least utilized.
Tutoring & education
Free tutoring for children, GED preparation, digital literacy courses, educational support programs. Directly tied to workforce readiness and long-term income trajectory. Almost universally unknown to members.
Meal delivery
Medically tailored meal programs for members with chronic conditions — diabetes, heart disease, kidney disease. Proven to reduce hospitalizations. Expensive to operate. Funded in the plan. Rarely claimed.
Transportation
Non-emergency medical transportation to appointments, rides to the pharmacy, to the specialist, to the lab. One of the most impactful benefits for reducing missed appointments — and one of the most consistently underused.
Retail memberships
Costco, Sam's Club, or similar warehouse memberships in select plans — enabling bulk food purchasing that reduces per-meal cost, improves nutrition, and stretches household budgets for working families.
Senior safety & wellness
Home safety equipment for seniors — grab bars, non-slip mats, alert devices, medication management tools. Prevents falls, reduces emergency care, keeps seniors independent longer. Budgeted for. Not used.
Dental & vision extras
Coverage beyond the standard Medicaid dental and vision floors — additional cleanings, eyeglass allowances, hearing aids, fluoride treatments. Improved dental health reduces systemic disease. Members don't know the extras exist.
The financial logic — why this is a cost savings argument, not a cost increase
What the MCO already did

The MCO negotiated value-added benefits into the plan. They priced them into the PMPM they receive from the state. They budgeted for utilization. In most markets, utilization came in far below that budget. The money is already spent. The outcome was never produced.

What benefit activation does

When the CHW activates benefits, members use them. Gym memberships reduce chronic disease risk. Meal delivery reduces hospitalizations. Transportation eliminates missed appointments. OTC benefits reduce out-of-pocket burden. Every benefit used produces a health outcome the MCO was trying to achieve with that investment.

The net effect on the MCO

Quality scores improve. HEDIS measures close. ED utilization drops. PMPM falls. The MCO was paying for these outcomes — and not getting them. Benefit activation is the mechanism that delivers what was already budgeted for. It is not a new line item. It is the ROI on an existing investment.

How the CHW activates benefits — at the individual level

The CHW learns the specific benefit package of every MCO serving their population. Not generically — specifically. Which OTC card vendor. Which gym network. Which transportation broker. Which meal program. How to enroll. What documentation is required. What the deadline is.

Then the CHW sits with each member and walks through it personally. In plain language. In their language if needed. Until the member understands what they have and how to use it. Then the CHW follows up to make sure they did.

This is not scalable through a brochure or a phone tree. It requires a trusted human relationship. That is what the CHW model provides — and what no automated outreach system can replicate.

How the CHW activates benefits — at the community level

Beyond individual activation, the CHW convenes community events specifically designed around insurance literacy. Not a generic health fair — a structured session where 40 members sit down, open their plan documentation, and the CHW walks the room through every benefit category together.

MCO representatives can be present. Benefit enrollment can happen on the spot. Questions get answered in real time. The CHW follows up individually with the members who need the most help navigating the process.

One event. Forty members. Hundreds of benefits activated. Zero new budget required from the MCO.

The argument for every MCO in every market You have already paid for these benefits. They are sitting in the plan right now — budgeted, contracted, and unclaimed. The CHW model activates them. When members use their gym membership, their chronic disease risk drops. When they use transportation, they make it to their appointment. When they use meal delivery, their A1C improves. When they enroll in prenatal support, maternal outcomes get better. None of this costs the MCO a dollar more than they already committed. It converts sunk cost into documented return — improving quality scores, reducing utilization, and producing the outcomes the plan was designed to deliver. The CHW is not an expense. The CHW is the activation key to the investment you already made.

The Workforce Pipeline

The most powerful outcome story in Medicaid.

A member who enters this pipeline dependent on public coverage exits with employer-sponsored insurance, a living wage, and no Medicaid claim. That is the story Anthem presents to CMS. That is what no other managed care investment produces.

Exposure
Real-world site observation · SDOH screening · CHW assigned Day 1
Stage 1
›

What happens: Participants are introduced to skilled trades through real-world observation on the active KG construction site — before any classroom requirement. SDOH screening completed. CHW assigned from Day 1. No credential needed to enter.

Anthem's role: Anthem members identified, enrolled, and attributed to Jamie's practice. Health gaps and barriers documented from the first CHW contact. The pipeline generates reportable outcomes from this moment forward.

What happens: Every identified barrier is addressed systematically. Health gaps closed at the clinic. Childcare secured. Transportation navigated. Anthem's role: Jamie closes HEDIS gaps during readiness visits. The CHW activates every benefit Anthem already paid for. Every Anthem service the member is entitled to is put to work.
